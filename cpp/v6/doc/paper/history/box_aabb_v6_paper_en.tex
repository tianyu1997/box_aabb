\begin{abstract}
This paper introduces \textsc{SafeBoxForest}~(SBF), a certified
box-cover and multi-query planning framework for high-degree-of-freedom
serial manipulators.
At its core is a Lifelong Envelope Cache Tree~(LECT), where
\emph{lifelong} denotes a persistent, kinematics-keyed cache that
survives across processes and obstacle layouts and is invalidated only
by changes in robot kinematics.
LECT unifies a three-stage erosion chain---endpoint iAABB,
link iAABB, and box safety---into a single reusable certificate;
interval forward kinematics, an analytical critical-point solver, and a
Krawczyk verifier all emit the same epiAABB intermediate, so cached
evidence remains valid when the upstream solver is upgraded or the
scene changes.
Building on LECT, SBF couples sparse voxel collision filtering,
connectivity-driven coordinated multi-tree growth, and multi-level
coarsening into a sub-second offline build that is directly consumable
by graph search or a Graph of Convex Sets~(GCS) corridor; online
queries run a Dijkstra--GCS--RRT competition followed by a five-step
path optimiser.
On the 7-DOF KUKA IIWA14 in the Marcucci 16-obstacle scene, SBF builds
a certified box forest in $2.23$\,s (median over 10 seeds), answers
the five benchmark queries with $100\%$ success rate and a $0.279$\,s
median latency. For \emph{producing a GCS-ready cover with comparable
end-to-end query connectivity on the same hardware}, the SBF backbone
is built roughly two orders of magnitude faster than the 8-region
IRIS-NP cover (${\sim}602$\,s in our environment vs.\ $2.23$\,s for
SBF); the comparison is asymmetric in unit (3{,}500 axis-aligned
boxes vs.\ 8 polytopes) and is discussed in detail in
\Cref{sec:exp_baselines}. Path quality is competitive: raw SBF paths are
$8.9\%$ longer than IRIS-NP+GCS in $L_2$, while feeding the SBF
backbone into a local GCS post-optimiser closes that gap; running the
same random-shortcut post-optimiser on the IRIS-NP+GCS output (R3-D2)
shrinks its paths by only $\le 1.4\%$ (median ratio $1.000$ over the
five benchmark queries), so SBF's residual gap is intrinsic to the
raw axis-aligned cover, not an unfair smoothing budget. A formal
sound-coarsening guarantee ensures that tight-channel envelopes
never propagate to the certified output, and a $4$-obstacle stress
case is reported separately as a known failure mode caused by the
axis-aligned approximation.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Sampling-based planning~\cite{lavalle2006planning,lavalle1998rapidly,%
kavraki1996probabilistic} remains the dominant choice for high-DOF
manipulators, yet its online latency and path quality are often limited
by collision-checking cost and local search behaviour.
The Graph of Convex Sets~(GCS) framework~\cite{marcucci2024motion}
offers an alternative by optimising directly over a graph of convex
free-space regions.
In practice, however, the bottleneck shifts upstream\,\cite{deits2015computing,werner2024fast}: before GCS can be
exploited, one must first construct a region set that is both safe and
inexpensive to maintain.
This requirement is particularly restrictive in assembly, warehousing,
and human--robot interaction scenarios, where obstacle layouts change
frequently while the robot kinematics remain fixed.
Under such conditions, a practical front-end must provide certified
safety, efficient construction, and cross-scene reuse rather than
optimise each region in isolation.

Existing methods do not satisfy these requirements jointly.
IRIS constructs large convex regions in the workspace, whereas IRIS-NP
and its faster variants extend region generation into configuration
space~\cite{deits2015computing,werner2024fast,werner2024irisze}.
Related convex-cover approaches further improve region quality or graph
compactness~\cite{amice2024clique,werner2024vcd}; however, repeated
execution under scene changes remains expensive, and certified reusable
geometric evidence is still scarce.
Sampling planners and trajectory optimisers can answer online queries
but do not build a persistent certified free-space representation for
subsequent tasks~\cite{lavalle1998rapidly,kuffner2000rrt,
gammell2020batch,ratliff2009chomp,schulman2014motion,
toussaint2017newton}.
The central challenge is therefore not only path search, but also how
geometric evidence about free space is accumulated, certified, and
reused.

SafeBoxForest~(SBF) is built on the observation that the mapping from a
configuration-space box to a conservative workspace link envelope is
governed by the robot kinematics and can therefore be cached
independently of any particular obstacle scene.
We use the term \emph{lifelong} in this paper in an operational sense:
\textbf{lifelong} = persistent across processes and obstacle scenes,
invalidated only by a change in the robot DH-hash. The envelope cache
therefore survives across processes and obstacle layouts, and is
invalidated only by changes in robot kinematics (DH parameters).
SBF stores these reusable envelopes in a Lifelong Envelope Cache Tree
(LECT), combines conservative interval enclosures with tighter boundary
analysis and certified verification, and constructs a box forest via
collision filtering, coordinated growth, and hierarchical coarsening.
The resulting structure supports two complementary modes: an offline
phase that builds a reusable free-space cover, and an online phase that
performs efficient box localisation, connectivity reasoning, and path
refinement.
When the scene changes, only the affected portion of the forest must be
repaired.

\textbf{The main contributions of this paper are as follows:}
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
  \item \emph{Unified envelope theory and Pareto characterisation.}
  We establish an erosion--dilation duality between epiAABB and
  LinkIAABB (Theorems~\ref{thm:erosion},\,\ref{thm:certify} and
  Proposition~\ref{prop:radius_lift}) that reduces the capsule-vs-AABB
  collision test to a 6-tuple of scalar bounds plus a single radius
  dilation, and provide a unified Pareto comparison of four endpoint
  sources (IFK / CritSample / Analytical / GCPC) and three downstream
  envelopes (LinkIAABB / LinkIAABB-Grid / Hull16-Grid) under a common
  intermediate representation (Tables~\ref{tab:epi_compare},\,\ref{tab:env_compare};
  see also the tightness ordering of \Cref{prop:env_order}).

  \item \emph{Lifelong Envelope Cache Tree with concurrent grower.}
  LECT couples dual safe/tight channels, Z4 rotational-symmetry caching,
  lazy materialisation, and cross-process mmap incremental save
  (\Cref{sec:lect_lazy}; FFB termination proof \Cref{prop:ffb};
  complexity \Cref{tab:lect_complexity}) with a coordinated multi-tree
  grower (connectivity-driven scheduling, opposite-face snapping,
  snapshot thread pool, in-place RRT-Connect, query-endpoint seed
  bridging, post-connect densification, \Cref{sec:grower_concurrency}).
  A \emph{sound coarsening} guarantee (\Cref{thm:coarsen_safe}) ensures
  the tight channel never compromises the certified output.

  \item \emph{Box-corridor interface to GCS}: the coarsened forest acts
  as a GCS front-end, reducing single-query GCS solve time from
  ${\sim}90$\,s to sub-second (\Cref{sec:exp_gcs_corridor}).

  \item \emph{Open-source IIWA14 validation.}
  Under the canonical 10-seed protocol of
  \Cref{tab:baselines_v2}, SBF builds a certified box forest
  (median ${\sim}3{,}500$ boxes) in $2.23$\,s (median; IQR
  $[1.61,\,2.49]$\,s), answers the 5 benchmark queries with $100\%$
  SR and a $0.279$\,s median query, and is roughly an order of
  magnitude faster than IRIS-NP on the same hardware (an 8-region
  IRIS-NP cover took ${\sim}602$\,s in our environment, with the
  worst-case anchor region alone reaching ${\sim}210$\,s, see
  \Cref{sec:exp_baselines}).
  Path quality is reported in two stages:
  raw SBF paths sit within $8.9\%$ of IRIS-NP+GCS in $L_2$ length;
  feeding the SBF backbone into a local GCS post-optimiser
  (\Cref{sec:exp_gcs_corridor}) closes that gap. We do not yet apply
  the same post-optimiser to the IRIS-NP+GCS output, so an absolute
  ``shorter than IRIS-NP+GCS'' claim is left to future work.
\end{enumerate}

Experiments on high-DOF manipulators in cluttered scenes show that SBF
reduces the cost of certified region construction and repeated planning
queries while maintaining consistent planning performance.

\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}

\subsection{GCS and Region Generation}

GCS~\cite{marcucci2024motion} formulates motion planning as an
optimisation problem over a graph of convex free-space regions, so the
quality of the final planner is tightly coupled to that of the upstream
region generator.
IRIS~\cite{deits2015computing} produces large convex regions via
iterative inflation, and IRIS-NP together with its faster
variants~\cite{werner2024fast,werner2024irisze} extends this idea to
configuration space.
Clique-cover and task-oriented convex-decomposition methods further
improve graph compactness or task relevance~\cite{amice2024clique,
werner2024vcd}, and IRIS-NP itself was first formalised as a nonlinear
program by Petersen and Tedrake~\cite{petersen2023growing}.
Recent extensions broaden the GCS framework beyond Euclidean
configuration spaces~\cite{cohn2023noneuclidean} and explore learned or
flow-based samplers as an alternative to deterministic
region-growing~\cite{wu2024flowgcs}.
Most of these approaches prioritise the geometry of individual regions;
in contrast, SBF emphasises reusable certified geometric evidence and
efficient coverage under repeated scene changes.

\emph{Differentiator versus the fast IRIS-NP variants.}
The accelerated IRIS-NP family~\cite{werner2024fast,werner2024irisze}
shortens the per-region growth loop and is the closest contemporary
work in spirit. SBF differs along three orthogonal axes:
(i)~\emph{lifelong cache:} kinematics-keyed boxes are memory-mapped
and reused across scenes, so amortised cost per scene drops as the
cache warms;
(ii)~\emph{dual channel:} the same forest serves both fast graph
search and a GCS corridor (\Cref{sec:exp_gcs_corridor}); and
(iii)~\emph{sub-second build:} the certified backbone is built in
${\sim}2.23$\,s on the IIWA14 + 16-obstacle scene
(\Cref{tab:baselines_v2}), enabling lifelong re-use within a single
planning episode rather than a one-shot precompute.

A complementary line of work—\emph{approximate cell
decomposition}~\cite{lozanoperez1983spatial,brooks1985subdivision}
and its modern interval/octree
variants—partitions $\Cfree$ into axis-aligned cells without convex
optimisation. SBF can be viewed as a high-DOF, certified extension of
this tradition: cells are kinematics-keyed, persistently cached, and
coarsened into a GCS-compatible adjacency graph rather than discarded
after the search.

\subsection{Certified Envelopes and Interval Methods}

Interval arithmetic provides rigorous enclosures of nonlinear image
sets~\cite{moore2009introduction,jaulin2001applied}, and interval-based
kinematics has been applied to workspace analysis and geometric
certification in robotics~\cite{merlet2004solving,merlet2006interval}.
Classical collision libraries and bounding-volume hierarchies---such as
FCL, OBB-trees, and GJK-based routines---are effective for
single-configuration queries~\cite{pan2012fcl,gottschalk1996obbtree,
gilbert1988fast} but do not maintain a scene-independent cache of
certified range-level information.
SBF adopts interval-style conservatism as its certification basis and
augments it with tighter envelope construction and persistent reuse at
the box level.

\subsection{Multi-Query Planning and Cross-Scene Reuse}

Persistent planning structures such as PRM~\cite{kavraki1996probabilistic}
amortise search effort across multiple queries, but the reused object
is typically a roadmap built for a specific collision-checking regime
rather than a certified free-space decomposition.
When the environment changes, such methods commonly fall back on
renewed local collision tests without an explicit cache of reusable
geometric certificates.
SBF targets a different layer of reuse: it stores box-level envelope
evidence tied to the robot kinematics, so the same cache can serve
multiple scenes and enable local repair after obstacle perturbations.

\subsection{Sampling-Based Planning and Trajectory Optimization}

RRT, RRT-Connect, and asymptotically optimal variants such as RRT* and
BIT* explore configuration space through point-level
sampling~\cite{lavalle1998rapidly,kuffner2000rrt,karaman2011sampling,
gammell2020batch}.
Trajectory optimisers such as CHOMP, TrajOpt, and KOMO improve path
quality through local refinement~\cite{ratliff2009chomp,
schulman2014motion,toussaint2017newton}.
These approaches are complementary to SBF: they are effective online
search and refinement tools but do not directly address the construction
of a reusable certified region graph for GCS.
We report RRT-Connect and PRM as baselines in \Cref{sec:exp_baselines};
an empirical BIT* head-to-head is deferred (it requires a Drake-side
OMPL-style harness that is not currently part of our reproducibility
bundle).

\paragraph{Approximate cell decomposition and voxel safe corridors.}
Classical approximate cell decomposition (ACD) recursively subdivides
$\Cspace$ into axis-aligned cells, labels each as
\textsc{free}/\textsc{mixed}/\textsc{full}, and searches the resulting
adjacency graph~\cite{lozanoperez1983spatial,brooks1985subdivision}.
Voxel-based ``safe corridor'' planners adopt the same primitive in
workspace, building a sparse occupancy grid and inflating it to a
strip of safe cells along a candidate trajectory. SBF differs from
these pipelines in three respects: (i) cells are
\emph{certified} via the dual-channel envelope of \Cref{thm:certify}
rather than majority-voted from samples, (ii) the cache is
robot-keyed rather than scene-keyed, and (iii) the resulting forest
is directly consumable as a GCS corridor without further
densification. The voxel-grid envelope variant of
\Cref{sec:envelope_compare} ports the ACD primitive into our
certified pipeline.

\section{Preliminaries and Problem Statement}\label{sec:prelim}

\subsection{Configuration Space Formalisation}

\begin{definition}[Interval Box]
For a $D$-DOF revolute robot, an \emph{interval box} is
$\mathcal{B} = \prod_{i=1}^{D}[l_i, u_i] \subset \Real^D$ with
half-width vector $w(\mathcal{B})_i = (u_i - l_i)/2$.
Throughout the experimental sections (e.g.\ the ``width bin''
stratification of \Cref{tab:ep_width_profile,tab:m1_erosion,tab:m2_pareto})
the term \emph{width} refers to the per-joint full extent $u_i-l_i$
rather than the half-width.
\end{definition}

\emph{Link geometry.} We adopt a capsule link model: link $\ell$ is the
cylinder of radius $r_\ell \geq 0$ around the centre line segment
$L_\ell(q) = \overline{\bm{p}_{\ell-1}(q)\bm{p}_\ell(q)}$ (with hemispherical
end caps), where $\bm{p}_k(q)\in\Real^3$ is the position of joint $k$'s
origin under MDH forward kinematics. Capsules outer-bound the true link
geometry, so capsule-based collision refutation remains conservative
with respect to the original mesh.

\begin{definition}[Free Space]\label{def:cfree}
Given workspace obstacles $\calO = \{O_j\}_{j=1}^{M}$ (each an axis-aligned
bounding box, AABB),
$\Cfree = \{q \in \Real^D \mid \forall \ell, j,\;
(L_\ell(q) \oplus B_3(r_\ell)) \cap O_j = \emptyset\}$,
where $\oplus$ is the Minkowski sum and $B_3(r)\subset\Real^3$ is a
solid Euclidean ball of radius $r$.
\end{definition}

A box $\mathcal{B}$ is \emph{safe} iff $\mathcal{B} \subseteq \Cfree$.

\subsection{Problem Statement}\label{sec:problem}

\begin{assumption}[Robot model scope]\label{ass:robot_scope}
Throughout this paper we assume the robot is an open kinematic chain
with only revolute joints (no prismatic, no floating base, no closed
kinematic loops), each joint $i$ having a finite analytic interval
$q_i\!\in\![q_i^{\min},q_i^{\max}]$ on which a single MDH branch is
valid. The interval-arithmetic envelope construction
(\Cref{sec:ifk,sec:bnb}) and the LECT splitting strategy
(\Cref{sec:lect}) both require this assumption: prismatic axes change
the scaling of the IFK Jacobian; closed loops introduce coupled
constraints that break the per-joint product structure of $T_\ell^0$;
branch cuts inside an interval invalidate the monotonicity statistic of
\Cref{sec:bnb}. Extensions to these regimes are out of scope.
\end{assumption}

Given (i) a robot MDH chain with capsule radii
$\{r_\ell\}_{\ell=1}^{L_\text{act}}$, (ii) a workspace obstacle set
$\calO$, and (iii) a stream of query pairs
$\{(\bm{q}_s^{(t)},\bm{q}_g^{(t)})\}_{t=1}^{T}$ (arriving online,
possibly interleaved with scene changes), we solve a \emph{two-phase}
problem:

\textbf{P1 (offline cover).} Construct a \emph{safe box forest}
$\mathcal{F}=\{B_i\}_{i=1}^{N}\subset 2^{\Cfree}$ together with its
face-adjacency graph $G=(V,E)$ such that, for a substantial fraction of
typical queries, there exist $B_s,B_g\in\mathcal{F}$ with
$\bm{q}_s\in B_s,\,\bm{q}_g\in B_g$, and $B_s,B_g$ are connected in $G$.

\textbf{P2 (online query).} For each query, output a \emph{collision-free}
path $\pi\subset\Cfree$ joining $\bm{q}_s,\bm{q}_g$ that minimizes
$L_2$ length under a fixed time budget.

\textbf{Hard constraints.} (a) Each $B\in\mathcal{F}$ is formally certified
$B\subseteq\Cfree$; (b) all geometric evidence is persisted by robot
fingerprint is recommended (SipHash-2-4 with a fixed key, $\le 2^{-32}$
collision probability across $\sim 10^4$ robots indexed in our
cache), so changing only $\calO$ does not invalidate
boxes geometrically disjoint from the modified region.

\subsection{Modified DH Convention and Link iAABB}\label{sec:dh}

Using the Craig MDH convention~\cite{craig2005introduction},
$T_\ell^0(\mathbf{q}) = \prod_{i=1}^{\ell} A_i(q_i)$, and the link
endpoints $\bm{p}_{\ell-1},\bm{p}_\ell$ are read from the translation
columns of $T_{\ell-1}^0,T_\ell^0$.

\begin{definition}[Endpoint iAABB / epiAABB]\label{def:epiaabb}
For endpoint $\bm{p}_k$ and box $\mathcal{B}$,
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{epiAABB}_k(\mathcal{B})
:=\prod_{d\in\{x,y,z\}}
\Bigl[\min_{q\in\mathcal{B}} p_k^d(q),\;
\max_{q\in\mathcal{B}} p_k^d(q)\Bigr],
\end{equation}
written compactly as $E_k(\mathcal{B})\in\Real^6$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Link iAABB / LinkIAABB]\label{def:iaabb}
The iAABB of link $\ell$ on $\mathcal{B}$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:linkiaabb_def}
\iAABB_\ell(\mathcal{B})
:=\prod_{d\in\{x,y,z\}}
\Bigl[\min_{q,\,t}\bigl((1-t)p_{\ell-1}^d(q)+tp_\ell^d(q)\bigr) - r_\ell,\;
\;\cdot\;+r_\ell\Bigr],
\end{equation}
where the inner $\min/\max$ are over $q\in\mathcal{B}, t\in[0,1]$.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Inner vs full LinkIAABB]\label{rem:iaabb_inner}
We write $\iAABB_\ell^\circ(\mathcal{B})$ for the \emph{inner} box
defined by the centre-segment swept hull alone (i.e.\
\eqref{eq:linkiaabb_def} without the $\pm r_\ell$ pad). Then
$\iAABB_\ell(\mathcal{B}) = \iAABB_\ell^\circ(\mathcal{B})
\oplus B_\infty(r_\ell)$, and \Cref{prop:radius_lift} simply states
that $\iAABB_\ell^\circ(\mathcal{B})
= \mathrm{bbox}(E_{\ell-1}(\mathcal{B}), E_\ell(\mathcal{B}))$. The
radius $r_\ell$ is therefore added \emph{exactly once}; the apparent
double appearance in Def.~\ref{def:iaabb} and Prop.~\ref{prop:radius_lift}
is a notational artefact of folding the dilation into the definition.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Collision-Model Erosion Theory}\label{sec:erosion}

To convert epiAABB into a usable LinkIAABB for \Cref{def:cfree}, we
establish an \emph{erosion--dilation duality}: capsule-vs-AABB collision
is equivalent to centre-segment collision against \emph{radius-dilated
obstacles} (or, dually, against \emph{radius-eroded free space}).

\begin{theorem}[Collision-Model Erosion Duality]\label{thm:erosion}
Let link $\ell$ be a capsule of radius $r_\ell$ with centre segment
$L_\ell(q)$, and let $O_j$ be an axis-aligned bounding box.
Both $L_\ell(q)$ and $O_j$ are convex compact sets.
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:erosion}
(L_\ell(q)\oplus B_3(r_\ell))\cap O_j = \emptyset
\;\Longleftrightarrow\;
L_\ell(q)\cap (O_j\oplus B_3(r_\ell)) = \emptyset.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
This is the standard Minkowski-sum duality for closed convex sets
(\cite{ericson2004real}, Ch.~4). For completeness:
let $A=L_\ell(q)$, $B=O_j$ and $r=r_\ell$. We show both directions.

\emph{($\Rightarrow$)} Suppose $(A\oplus B_3(r))\cap B = \emptyset$
but $A\cap (B\oplus B_3(r)) \neq \emptyset$. Then there exist $a\in A$,
$b\in B$, and $u\in B_3(r)$ such that $a = b+u$, i.e.\ $\|a-b\|_2\le r$.
Since $B_3(r)$ is centrally symmetric, $u'=-u$ also lies in $B_3(r)$, so $a+u'=b\in B$
and $a+u'\in A\oplus B_3(r)$, contradicting disjointness.

\emph{($\Leftarrow$)} Symmetric: any witness pair $a\in A$, $b\in B$
with $\|a-b\|_2\le r$ yields $a\in B\oplus B_3(r)$ and
$b\in A\oplus B_3(r)$ simultaneously, so disjointness on either side
precludes such a pair (cf.\ \cite{ericson2004real} Ch.~4).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[LinkIAABB Contains the Swept Dilated Segment]
\label{prop:iaabb_contains}
For all $q\in\mathcal{B}$,
$\;L_\ell(q)\oplus B_3(r_\ell) \subseteq \iAABB_\ell(\mathcal{B})$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For any $q\in\mathcal{B},\,t\in[0,1]$, write
$x(q,t) = (1-t)\bm{p}_{\ell-1}(q)+t\bm{p}_\ell(q)$. The inner $\min/\max$
in \Cref{def:iaabb} range over all such $(q,t)$, hence (before the outer
$\pm r_\ell$ dilation) $x(q,t)$ is contained in the inner box. The outer
$\pm r_\ell$ dilation produces an $\ell^\infty$-ball around the inner
box; since $\|y\|_2\le r_\ell$ implies $\max_d|y_d|\le r_\ell$, we have
$B_3(r_\ell)\subseteq B_\infty(r_\ell)$, and therefore the swept dilated
segment is contained in $\iAABB_\ell(\mathcal{B})$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Box-Level Collision Refutation]\label{thm:certify}
If $\iAABB_\ell(\mathcal{B})\cap O_j = \emptyset$ for every link $\ell$
and obstacle $O_j$, then $\mathcal{B}\subseteq\Cfree$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For any $q\in\mathcal{B},\ell,j$, \Cref{prop:iaabb_contains} gives
$L_\ell(q)\oplus B_3(r_\ell)\subseteq \iAABB_\ell(\mathcal{B})$, which
is disjoint from $O_j$. Hence the dilated segment is disjoint from
$O_j$, and by \Cref{def:cfree}, $q\in\Cfree$.
\end{proof}

\Cref{thm:certify} states the safety contract of SBF: \emph{all formal
safety evidence reduces to coordinate-wise $\min/\max$ of an epiAABB
plus a scalar radius constant}, so the LECT cache unit is the epiAABB
rather than the LinkIAABB.

\begin{remark}[Tightness Bounds]\label{rem:tightness}
Compared with the exact swept dilated set
$\bigcup_{q\in\mathcal{B}} L_\ell(q)\oplus B_3(r_\ell)$, the
over-approximation in $\iAABB_\ell$ has three sources:
(i)~the \emph{wrapping effect} of interval multiplication (IFK source);
(ii)~outer-bounding the swept hull by an axis-aligned box (hull$\to$AABB
loss); (iii)~replacing the Euclidean ball $B_3(r_\ell)$ by a per-axis
$\pm r_\ell$ pad ($\ell^\infty\!\supseteq\!\ell^2$). Source (iii) is
universal; (ii) is partly recovered by Hull16 envelopes
(\Cref{sec:envelope_compare}); (i) appears only for IFK
(\Cref{tab:ep_width_profile}).
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Radius-Erosion Additivity]\label{prop:radius_lift}
Given $E_{\ell-1}(\mathcal{B}),E_\ell(\mathcal{B})$,
\begin{equation}
\iAABB_\ell(\mathcal{B})
= \mathrm{bbox}\bigl(E_{\ell-1}(\mathcal{B}),E_\ell(\mathcal{B})\bigr)
\oplus B_\infty(r_\ell),
\end{equation}
where $B_\infty(r)=[-r,r]^3$. That is, LinkIAABB is obtained from
epiAABB by a \emph{constant per-axis dilation}, with no additional
kinematics evaluation.
\end{proposition}

\Cref{prop:radius_lift} is the algebraic foundation for the modular
envelope pipeline of \Cref{eq:pipeline}: different endpoint sources only
affect epiAABB tightness, while every downstream LinkIAABB / grid /
hull variant shares the same 6-component intermediate, so LECT need not
regenerate cached entries when a source is upgraded.

\section{Certified Envelope Computation}\label{sec:envelope}

SBF adopts a modular two-layer pipeline. Four interchangeable
\emph{endpoint sources} feed three \emph{envelope representations}, and
the common endpoint-iAABB intermediate format decouples endpoint
generation from downstream rasterisation:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pipeline}
  \text{Endpoint source}
  \;\xrightarrow{\;E_k\;}\;
  \text{Envelope representation}.
\end{equation}
The source side comprises IFK, CritSample, Analytical, GCPC, and an
MC baseline,
whereas the representation side comprises LinkIAABB,
LinkIAABB-Grid, and Hull16-Grid.
For each endpoint $k$, the endpoint iAABB
$E_k(\mathcal{B}) = [\mathrm{lo}_x, \mathrm{lo}_y, \mathrm{lo}_z,
\mathrm{hi}_x, \mathrm{hi}_y, \mathrm{hi}_z]$ of
\Cref{def:epiaabb} is the six-tuple stored per endpoint (the parent
box $\mathcal{B}$ is left implicit when unambiguous), so the total
storage is $n_{\text{endpoints}} \times 6$ floating-point values. A
link iAABB is derived from an adjacent endpoint pair via
\Cref{prop:radius_lift}:
$\iAABB_\ell = \mathrm{bbox}(E_{k_{\ell-1}}, E_{k_\ell})
\oplus B_\infty(r_\ell)$.

\subsection{epiAABB Source Pipelines Compared}\label{sec:epi_pipelines}

The four endpoint sources correspond to four distinct geometric--algebraic
trade-offs, summarised below.

\textbf{IFK (Interval Forward Kinematics).}
Scalar joint multiplications in the DH chain are replaced by interval
multiplications. $\mathcal{O}(D)$ time; conservatism follows from the
inclusion-monotonicity of interval arithmetic~\cite{moore2009introduction,
jaulin2001applied}; tightness is limited by the \emph{wrapping effect}
and degrades on wide boxes (in the $[0.20,0.50]$\,rad bin of
\Cref{tab:ep_width_profile}, IFK volume is $3.4\times$ that of GCPC).

\textbf{CritSample (Boundary Critical Sampling).}
Enumerates boundary values and $k\pi/2$ crossings per joint and combines
them into $\prod_j |C_j|$ candidate configurations evaluated by scalar
FK. $1.7\!-\!3.4\times$ tighter than IFK at microsecond cost, but the
per-joint $C_j$ misses multi-joint coupling criticals, producing small
\emph{under-approximations} that are isolated by the dual-channel
protocol (\Cref{rem:safety}).

\textbf{Analytical (Five-Phase Solver).}
Reduces 1D/2D slices to sinusoidal or bilinear trigonometric polynomials
(Propositions~\ref{prop:sinusoid}--\ref{prop:bilinear}) solved
exactly by atan2 and companion-matrix eigenvalues, then covers
$|F|\geq 3$ free sets by coordinate descent; a Krawczyk verifier
(\Cref{eq:krawczyk}) guarantees
$\widehat{\iAABB}\supseteq\iAABB^{\text{true}}$. Tightness is within
$<2\%$ of a million-sample MC baseline, but the per-box cost is
$\sim$10\,ms, $10^4\times$ slower than IFK.

\textbf{GCPC (Geometric Critical-Point Cache).}
Pre-computes the Analytical result once per robot, organised by link in
a KD-tree that exploits $q_0$ rotational invariance and $q_1$ $\pi$-periodic
symmetry. Online queries are reduced to KD-tree range lookup and endpoint
reconstruction, matching Analytical in volume but dropping to
$\sim$8\,ms per call, with a much flatter dependence on box width
(\Cref{tab:ep_width_profile}).

\textbf{MC (Monte Carlo, reference only).}
Width-proportional sample density
$n_\text{mc} = \mathrm{clip}(\rho\bar{w},\,10^3,\,10^7)$; provides a
\emph{tight lower bound} for benchmarking but is not a certified
envelope.

\subsection{Interval FK (IFK)}\label{sec:ifk}

IFK replaces scalar joint values in the DH chain by interval-valued
counterparts and multiplies them sequentially:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:chain}
  [\bm{T}^{0:k}] = [\bm{T}^{0:k-1}] \otimes [\bm{T}_k],
\end{equation}
and endpoint positions are read directly from the translational entries
of $[\bm{T}^{0:\ell}]$. By the inclusion-monotonicity property of
interval arithmetic~\cite{moore2009introduction}, the resulting
envelopes are provably conservative: over-approximation may cause false
rejection, but never false acceptance. The wrapping effect introduced by
chain multiplication affects tightness but not soundness.

\emph{Active-link filtering.}
For each link $\ell$, define the nominal length
$L_\ell = \sqrt{a_\ell^2 + d_\ell^2}$ from its DH parameters. Links
with $L_\ell < 0.1\,$m are treated as zero-length and discarded. Only
$L_{\text{act}} \leq L$ active links are retained
(IIWA14: $L_{\text{act}} = 4$), reducing both storage and
collision-checking cost.

\emph{Incremental forward kinematics.}
When a box is split along dimension $d$, the prefix
$[\bm{T}^{0:d-1}]$ is shared by both child boxes, so only the suffix
$[\bm{T}^{d:D}]$ needs recomputation. On a 7-DOF chain this saves about
43\% of FK cost.

\subsection{Boundary Critical Sampling (CritSample)}\label{sec:critsample}

For each joint, the critical-angle set is
$C_j = \{q_j^-, q_j^+\} \cup \{k\pi/2 \in (q_j^-, q_j^+)\}$.
All associated DH transforms are precomputed, eliminating trigonometric
evaluation from the hot path. On the IIWA14 benchmark in
\Cref{sec:exp_envelope}, CritSample reduces envelope volume to
56.8\%--28.7\% of IFK while remaining in the 8.5--40.4\,$\mu$s range.
Analytical and GCPC produce numerically aligned volumes, with GCPC
delivering lower runtime.

\subsection{Analytical Critical-Point Solver}\label{sec:analytical}

\subsubsection{Trigonometric Structure of the DH Chain}

For a revolute joint $j$, the DH transform is affine in
$(\cos q_j, \sin q_j)$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dh_affine}
  \bm{T}_j(q_j) = \bm{M}_j^{(c)}\cos q_j + \bm{M}_j^{(s)}\sin q_j + \bm{M}_j^{(0)},
\end{equation}
where $\bm{M}_j^{(c)}$, $\bm{M}_j^{(s)}$, and $\bm{M}_j^{(0)}$ depend
only on the DH parameters.

\begin{proposition}[Univariate Sinusoid]\label{prop:sinusoid}
If all joints except $j$ are fixed, each endpoint coordinate reduces to
$p_{\ell,d}(q_j) = A_j\cos q_j + B_j\sin q_j + C_j$, and therefore has
exactly two critical points per $2\pi$ period:
$q_j^* = \mathrm{atan2}(B_j, A_j)$ and $q_j^* + \pi$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Bilinear Trigonometric Form]\label{prop:bilinear}
If all joints except $(i,j)$ are fixed, each endpoint coordinate can be
written as
$p_{\ell,d}(q_i, q_j) = \sum_{m=1}^{9} a_m \phi_m(q_i, q_j)$,
where
$\{\phi_m\} = \{c_i c_j, c_i s_j, s_i c_j, s_i s_j, c_i, s_i, c_j, s_j, 1\}$.
Setting $\nabla_{(q_i,q_j)} p_{\ell,d} = \bm{0}$ and applying the
Weierstrass substitution $t_j = \tan(q_j/2)$ reduces the problem to a
polynomial in $t_j$ of degree at most eight, which can be solved
exactly through companion-matrix eigenanalysis.
\end{proposition}

\subsubsection{Direct Coefficient Extraction from the DH Chain}

The composite transform factorises into prefix and suffix blocks:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:prefix_suffix}
  \bm{T}^{0:\ell} = \underbrace{\bm{T}^{0:j-1}}_{\text{prefix}}
  \cdot \bm{T}_j(q_j) \cdot
  \underbrace{\bm{T}^{j+1:\ell}}_{\text{suffix}}.
\end{equation}
Combining \eqref{eq:dh_affine} with the translational column of the
product yields the 1D coefficients $(A_d, B_d, C_d)$ directly, at the
cost of about 30 multiplications and zero FK calls. The 2D coefficients
for a joint pair $(i,j)$ are extracted analogously in about 120
multiplications. This direct extraction replaces QR fitting and reduces
the combined Phase 1+2 coefficient computation from 22.9\,ms to
14.4\,ms, a 37\% speedup.

\subsubsection{Five-Phase Solver}

The solver enumerates candidate extrema according to the dimension of
the free set:

\emph{Phase 0} --- vertex enumeration ($|F| = 0$).
\emph{Phase 1} --- edge critical points ($|F| = 1$) via direct 1D DH
coefficient extraction and sinusoidal solving.
\emph{Phase 2} --- face critical points ($|F| = 2$) via direct 2D DH
coefficient extraction and degree-$\leq 8$ companion-matrix solving.
\emph{Phase 2.5} --- pair-constrained critical points
($q_i + q_j = k\pi/2$).
\emph{Phase 3} --- interior coordinate descent ($|F| \geq 3$), with a
dual-Phase-3 fusion strategy for better basin-switching robustness.

\begin{theorem}[Face-$k$ Completeness for $k \leq 2$, with BnB closure for $k\geq 3$]\label{thm:face}
\emph{Lower-dimensional cases.} If the global extremiser of
$f(q)=p_{\ell,d}(q)$ on $\mathcal{B}$ has a free set of size
$|F|\leq 2$, the analytical solver of \Cref{sec:analytical}
(Phases~0/1/2) finds a configuration with equal or better objective
value.
\emph{Higher-dimensional fallback.} For $|F|\geq 3$, completeness is
\textbf{not} asserted by the analytical solver alone --- Phase~3’s
dual coordinate-descent fusion is a heuristic local optimiser. The
certified envelope bounds are instead closed by the Krawczyk
branch-and-bound verifier of \Cref{sec:bnb} (with the non-singularity
fallback of paragraph~A3 below): every $|F|\geq 3$ cell that the
analytical solver leaves uncertified is bisected and re-verified
until either (a)~Krawczyk certifies a unique critical point and the
endpoint envelope is updated, (b)~Krawczyk certifies that no
critical point exists in the cell (so the cell’s contribution is
closed by the boundary), or (c)~the depth budget $d_{\max}$ is
reached and the cell is conservatively marked \textsc{Unknown},
feeding the conservative clamping of \Cref{thm:certify}. In all three
sub-cases the resulting endpoint iAABB remains an over-approximation,
so the certification chain is sound regardless of Phase~3’s local
outcome.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $f(q) = p_{\ell,d}(q)$ denote coordinate $d$ of the endpoint of
link $\ell$ as a function of the joint vector $q \in \mathcal{B}$. The
DH chain gives
$f(q) = e_d^\top T_1(q_1) \cdots T_D(q_D)\,b$,
where each $T_i(q_i)$ is a $4{\times}4$ DH transform.

\emph{Case $|F| = 0$ (vertices).}
All joints lie on the box boundary. There are exactly $2^D$ vertices,
and Phase 0 evaluates all of them, so the extremiser is found.

\emph{Case $|F| = 1$ (edges).}
Fix all joints except $q_j$ at boundary values. Then
$f_j(\theta) = a\cos\theta + b\sin\theta + c$, where $a$, $b$, and $c$
are obtained directly from the DH chain. The condition
$f'_j(\theta) = 0$ gives $\tan\theta = b/a$, so there are at most two
critical points in $[q_j^-, q_j^+]$ in addition to the two endpoints.
Phase 1 solves this case exactly.

\emph{Case $|F| = 2$ (faces).}
Fix all joints except $q_i$ and $q_j$ at boundary values. Expanding the
DH product yields a trigonometric polynomial in $(q_i, q_j)$. Setting
$\partial f/\partial q_i = 0$ and $\partial f/\partial q_j = 0$, then
substituting $c_i = \cos q_i$ and $s_i = \sin q_i$ with
$c_i^2 + s_i^2 = 1$, produces a polynomial system whose real roots can
be recovered by companion-matrix eigendecomposition. Phase 2 enumerates
all real roots inside the box; hence every interior critical point on a
2-face is found.

Since each case exhaustively enumerates all critical points on the
corresponding face, any global extremiser with $|F| \leq 2$ is found by
the combined Phase 0/1/2 enumeration.
\end{proof}

\subsubsection{IFK Certification}

Clamping guarantees
$\widehat{\iAABB}_\ell \supseteq \iAABB^{\mathrm{true}}_\ell$
independently of Phase 3 completeness.

\subsection{Geometric Critical-Point Cache (GCPC)}\label{sec:gcpc}

GCPC amortises the analytical solver across all queries for a fixed
robot through an eight-stage query pipeline.\
\emph{One-time precomputation:} for each active link, interior critical
configurations are enumerated over the full joint range by coordinate
descent and stored in a KD-tree indexed by link.\
\emph{Symmetry reduction:} $q_0$ elimination and the period-$\pi$
symmetry of $q_1$ halve the required storage.

Given a box $B$, GCPC queries proceed through the following stages:
\textbf{A.} cache lookup by KD-tree range query and symmetry recovery;
\textbf{B.} boundary $k\pi/2$ enumeration;
\textbf{C.} 1D DH direct extraction with closed-form arctangent solving and
axis-improvability pruning;
\textbf{D.} 2D companion-matrix solve with the same pruning;
\textbf{D$\frac{1}{2}$.} background lookup for $k\pi/2$ cases;
\textbf{E.} pair-constrained 1D solving for
$q_i + q_j = k\pi/2$;
\textbf{F.} pair-constrained 2D solving with incremental chain
recomputation; and
\textbf{G.} multi-start interior coordinate descent.
The strong prior bounds from Stage A make aggressive pruning effective,
greatly reducing the number of expensive FK calls. GCPC produces
endpoint iAABBs at $0.89{\times}$ the cost of the analytical solver,
with nearly identical volumes
($0.121$--$0.123$\,m$^3$, about 1.4\% difference). The cache is
scene-independent and can be serialised to disk.

\subsection{Certified BnB Verifier with Monotonicity Reduction}\label{sec:bnb}

For $|F| \geq 3$, a Krawczyk interval-Newton verifier combined with
monotonicity-based dimension reduction closes the completeness gap of
Phase 3.

\subsubsection{Krawczyk Verification}

For a free joint $j$, let
$f(q_j) = -A_j\sin q_j + B_j\cos q_j$ denote the derivative. Its
Krawczyk operator is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:krawczyk}
  K(X) = \hat{x} - Yf(\hat{x}) + (1 - Yf'(X))(X - \hat{x}),
\end{equation}
where $\hat{x} = \mathrm{mid}(X)$ and $Y = 1/f'(\hat{x})$.
The inclusion $K(X) \subset \mathrm{int}(X)$ certifies a unique root in
$X$, whereas $K(X) \cap X = \emptyset$ certifies that no root exists.

\paragraph{Non-singularity precondition and BnB fallback (A3).}
The Krawczyk operator in \eqref{eq:krawczyk} is well defined only when
$0\notin[f'(X)]$ (so that $Y=1/f'(\hat{x})$ exists and the contraction
factor $1-Yf'(X)$ is finite). When the interval enclosure of $f'$
crosses zero on the current cell---i.e. $0\in[f'(X)]$---we treat the
Krawczyk step as inconclusive, do \emph{not} return a unique-root
certificate, and instead bisect $X$ along its widest dimension and
push both halves back into the BnB queue. This guarantees that every
certificate emitted by the verifier comes from a sub-cell on which
$Y$ is finite, while preserving completeness in the limit (within the
depth budget $d_{\max}$, see \Cref{sec:lect_lazy}). When $d_{\max}$
is reached without resolution, the cell is conservatively marked
\textsc{Unknown} and excluded from the certified set, never
optimistically accepted.
The branch-and-bound engine bisects unresolved cells until all of them
are either certified or the depth budget is reached.

\subsubsection{Monotonicity-Based Dimension Reduction}

Partial derivatives are enclosed through the prefix-suffix factorisation
in \eqref{eq:prefix_suffix}:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mono_test}
  \left[\frac{\partial p_{\ell,d}}{\partial q_j}\right]
  \supseteq -[\alpha_{j,d}]\cdot[\sin X_j]
  + [\beta_{j,d}]\cdot[\cos X_j].
\end{equation}
If $0 \notin [\partial p_{\ell,d}/\partial q_j]$, then that dimension is
strictly monotone and its extremum must lie on the boundary.

\begin{theorem}[Monotone Corner Optimality]\label{thm:monotone}
If $p_{\ell,d}$ is strictly monotone in every free dimension, then its
global extremum lies at the corner determined by the gradient signs.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $f(q) = p_{\ell,d}(q)$ and suppose $\partial f / \partial q_i$ has a
constant sign on $\mathcal{B}$ for every free dimension $i \in F$.
Without loss of generality, consider maximisation. If
$\partial f / \partial q_i > 0$ on $\mathcal{B}$, then $f$ is strictly
increasing in $q_i$ and the maximum is attained at the upper bound
$q_i = q_i^+$. If $\partial f / \partial q_i < 0$, the maximum is
attained at the lower bound $q_i = q_i^-$. Since $\mathcal{B}$ is a
Cartesian product, fixing each free coordinate to its optimal boundary
value independently yields a corner point
$q^* \in \mathrm{corners}(\mathcal{B})$ with
$f(q^*) \geq f(q)$ for all $q \in \mathcal{B}$. The minimisation case is
symmetric.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Empirical Monotonicity Frequency]\label{rem:mono_freq}
For a DH chain with $n$ joints and box half-width $w$, the probability
that all dimensions are monotone increases as $w \to 0$. At
$w = 0.005\,$rad and $n = 7$, empirical measurement yields monotonicity
in more than 98.9\% of all box--link--coordinate instances, reducing
the verifier to two scalar FK evaluations per dimension. This is an
empirical observation about the IIWA14 chain in our scenes, not part
of the proof of \Cref{thm:monotone}.
\end{remark}

\subsection{LinkIAABB Envelope Pipelines Compared}\label{sec:envelope_compare}

By \Cref{prop:radius_lift}, given an epiAABB $E$, three distinct
LinkIAABB envelopes can be emitted, corresponding to different
geometry--storage--time trade-offs:

\textbf{(a) LinkIAABB (axis-aligned only).}
$\mathrm{Env}_a = \mathrm{bbox}(E_{\ell-1},E_\ell) \oplus B_\infty(r_\ell)$.
6 floats per link; AABB-vs-AABB intersection is six comparisons
$\mathcal{O}(1)$. Cheapest but loosest: contains the full endpoint AABB
hull plus an $\ell^\infty$ dilation, discarding fine detail along the
link axis.

\textbf{(b) LinkIAABB-Grid (axis-aligned + voxel grid).}
Rasterises $\mathrm{Env}_a$ at voxel size $\delta$ into sparse BitBricks
(\Cref{sec:voxel}); collision is a bitwise AND against pre-rasterised
obstacles, yielding \emph{shape-level} intersection. Storage
$\mathcal{O}(V/\delta^3)$ bits; query $\mathcal{O}(K)$ with $K$ hit
bricks $\ll N$. Finer $\delta$ tightens but inflates storage
(\Cref{tab:grid_sweep}).

\textbf{(c) Hull16-Grid (convex hull + voxel grid).}
Rasterises the 16-vertex convex hull
$\mathrm{Conv}(\partial E_{\ell-1}\cup\partial E_\ell)$ directly using
the turbo scanline algorithm of \Cref{sec:voxel}. Tighter than (b) by
recovering source-(ii) loss in \Cref{rem:tightness}: when a link moves
diagonally, the AABB contains large empty wedges that the convex hull
excludes. At $\delta=0.04$\,m the volume ratio improves from $96.5\%$ to
$77.8\%$ (\Cref{tab:grid_sweep}).

\begin{remark}[Envelope Tightness Ordering]\label{prop:env_order}
For the same epiAABB input the three envelopes satisfy
$\mathrm{vol}(\mathrm{Env}_c) \leq \mathrm{vol}(\mathrm{Env}_b)
\leq \mathrm{vol}(\mathrm{Env}_a)$:
$\mathrm{Env}_b$ is a grid outer-approximation of $\mathrm{Env}_a$
(monotone in $\delta$), and
$\mathrm{Conv}(\cdot)\subseteq\mathrm{bbox}(\cdot)$ gives
$\mathrm{Env}_c\subseteq\mathrm{Env}_b$.
\end{remark}

\section{Sparse Voxel BitMask}\label{sec:voxel}

\subsection{BitBrick Layout}

The workspace is partitioned into $8^3 = 512$-voxel BitBricks
(64 bytes, one cache line), addressed through spatial hashing
(FNV-1a).

\subsection{16-Point Convex Hull Rasterization}

Given a proximal box $B_1$ and a distal box $B_2$, the swept volume is
$S_\ell = \mathrm{Conv}(\mathrm{corners}(B_1) \cup \mathrm{corners}(B_2))$,
which has at most 16 vertices. The bounds of the interpolated cross
section $B(t) = (1-t)B_1 + tB_2$ vary linearly with $t$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bt_linear}
  \mathrm{lo}_a(t) = b_{1,\mathrm{lo}}^a + t\,\Delta\mathrm{lo}^a, \quad
  \mathrm{hi}_a(t) = b_{1,\mathrm{hi}}^a + t\,\Delta\mathrm{hi}^a.
\end{equation}
The turbo scanline algorithm exploits this linearity through an
\emph{YZ $t$-range solver} with $O(1)$ work per scanline, a
\emph{conservative $x$ fill} by endpoint extrema, and a
\emph{batch bit fill} implemented as precomputed 64-bit masks.
Brick processing is batched in $8{\times}8$ tiles; tiles provably
outside the convex hull are skipped entirely. The total complexity is
$O(n_y \cdot n_z)$ with $O(1)$ amortised work per scanline.

\subsection{Voxel Operations}

\emph{Collision:}
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
    \mathrm{collide}(G_R, G_O)
    &\Leftrightarrow \exists\,\bm{b}, k: \\
    &\quad (G_R[\bm{b}].\mathrm{words}[k] \mathbin{\&} \\
    &\qquad G_O[\bm{b}].\mathrm{words}[k]) \ne 0.
  \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
This reduces to 64-bit AND operations with early exit.

\emph{Lossless merge:}
\begin{equation}
  G_P[\bm{b}] = G_L[\bm{b}] \mathbin{|} G_R[\bm{b}].
\end{equation}
Unlike iAABB unions, voxel OR preserves the exact merged shape with zero
volume inflation.

\emph{Scene pre-rasterisation:}
Obstacle iAABBs are rasterised once into a shared voxel grid. Subsequent
collision queries then reduce to hashed grid intersections and wordwise
AND, yielding a measured 2272$\times$ speedup.

\section{LECT: Lifelong Envelope Cache Tree}\label{sec:lect}

\subsection{Architecture}

LECT is a lazy KD-tree over configuration space. Each node $v$ stores a
box $B_v$ and caches its link envelopes on demand:
\begin{equation}
  \iAABB_\ell(B_v) = \mathrm{bbox}\bigl([\bm{p}_{\ell-1}]_{B_v},
  [\bm{p}_\ell]_{B_v}\bigr) \oplus r_\ell.
\end{equation}
Its key properties are lazy evaluation, subtree-occupancy tracking, and
binary/mmap-based incremental persistence.
\emph{Occupancy.} A leaf $v$ is \emph{occupied} when its box $B_v$ has
already been claimed by a forest member or a worker tree; an internal
node is occupied iff its subtree-occupancy counter $\nu(v)$ saturates
the leaves below it (\Cref{sec:ffb_analysis}). FFB uses occupancy only
for early termination, never for safety certification.

\subsection{Split Strategies}

LECT supports three split criteria: round-robin
($\mathrm{depth} \bmod D$), widest-first, and best-tighten (default).
For a candidate split dimension $d$, the left and right child boxes are
probed to estimate the total envelope volume
$\sum_i V_i^L(d)$ and $\sum_i V_i^R(d)$, and the selected dimension is
the minimax choice:
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
    d^* &= \arg\min_d\; \mathrm{metric}(d), \\
    \mathrm{metric}(d)
      &= \max\!\Bigl\{\sum_i V_i^L(d),\; \sum_i V_i^R(d)\Bigr\}.
  \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
In implementation, the best split dimension is cached per tree depth so
that later nodes at the same depth can reuse it without repeated
evaluation. For highly anisotropic boxes, a multi-probe tightening
heuristic introduces a width penalty:
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
    \mathrm{score}_d
    &= \frac{V_L(d)+V_R(d)}{V_{\mathrm{parent}}} \\
    &\quad \times \left(\frac{w_{\max}}{w_d}\right)^{\gamma},
  \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
which discourages repeatedly splitting already narrow dimensions.

\subsection{Z4 Rotational Symmetry}

For robots with a $90^\circ$ symmetry at joint~0, such as the KUKA
IIWA14, LECT first canonicalises the joint-0 interval into a base sector
with period $T = \pi/2$:
\begin{equation}
  k = \left\lfloor q_0^{\mathrm{lo}}/T \right\rfloor,\quad
  [\hat q_0^{\mathrm{lo}},\hat q_0^{\mathrm{hi}}] =
  [q_0^{\mathrm{lo}}-kT,\;q_0^{\mathrm{hi}}-kT],
\end{equation}
and uses the canonical key
\begin{equation}
  K_{\mathrm{sym}} = \mathcal{H}\bigl(\hat q_0^{\mathrm{lo}},\hat q_0^{\mathrm{hi}},
  [l_j,u_j]_{j=1}^{D-1}\bigr)
\end{equation}
for endpoint-iAABB caching. On a cache hit, the stored canonical sector
is rotated back to the target sector indexed by $s \in \{0,1,2,3\}$.
Grid caches use the augmented key
$K_{\mathrm{grid}} = (K_{\mathrm{sym}} \ll 2)\,|\,s$ so that
non-canonical sectors are never rasterised twice.

When splitting along $q_0$, the algorithm preferentially selects the
nearest internal sector boundary $k\pi/2$ instead of the geometric
midpoint, which increases cache reuse across neighbouring sectors. In
the IFK mode, this four-sector symmetry cache combines with incremental
FK and partial inheritance to deliver a modest cold-cache speedup at
deeper tree levels (the warm-cache regime quickly saturates the
effect, so we report it qualitatively rather than as a single
speedup number).

\subsection{Incremental FK Optimization}

When the interval-FK state is valid, endpoint coordinates are extracted
directly from the cached prefix transforms, bypassing full endpoint
reconstruction. Links unaffected by the split inherit their endpoint
data directly from the parent node, reducing per-node FK cost by about
40--45\%.

\subsection{FFB: Find Free Box}

FFB follows a seed path and lazily splits the LECT until it finds a leaf
that is both collision-free and unoccupied. Subtree-occupancy counters
enable early termination when an entire subtree is already full.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Find Free Box (FFB)}\label{alg:ffb}
\footnotesize
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE LECT $T$, seed $\bm{q}$, obstacles $\mathcal{O}$,
  max depth $d_{\max}$, deadline $t_{\max}$
\ENSURE Free LECT node $v$, or $\emptyset$
\STATE $v \leftarrow \text{root}$; init running FK state and intervals
\WHILE{\TRUE}
  \IF{deadline exceeded (sampled every 64 steps)}
    \RETURN $\emptyset$
  \ENDIF
  \IF{$v$ is occupied}
    \IF{$v$ is leaf \AND $\mathrm{depth}(v) \geq d_{\max}$}
      \RETURN $\emptyset$
    \ENDIF
    \STATE Expand $v$ if leaf; descend to child $\ni \bm{q}$; update FK
  \ELSE
    \IF{$v$ has no cached envelope}
      \STATE Compute and cache envelope via incremental IFK
    \ENDIF
    \IF{$\mathrm{envelope}(v) \cap \mathcal{O} = \emptyset$}
      \RETURN $v$ \hfill\COMMENT{certified free}
    \ELSE
      \IF{$\mathrm{depth}(v) \geq d_{\max}$}
        \RETURN $\emptyset$
      \ENDIF
      \STATE Expand $v$ if leaf; descend to child $\ni \bm{q}$; update FK
    \ENDIF
  \ENDIF
\ENDWHILE
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Lazy Materialisation and Incremental mmap Save}\label{sec:lect_lazy}

LECT's node pool uses a capacity--length split layout with three
components: an in-memory writable buffer $V$ holding $n_\text{nodes}$
freshly allocated nodes; a memory-mapped read-only cache file $M$,
identified by a robot-kinematics fingerprint and holding $n_\text{disk}$
historical nodes; and a logical capacity
$\kappa = \max(n_\text{nodes}, n_\text{disk})$ that defines a globally
unique node-ID space $[0, \kappa)$.

\textbf{Lazy materialisation.}
At startup, LECT memory-maps the history file and registers its first
$n_\text{disk}$ slots into the ID space, while $V$ stays empty. Every
read first queries $V$ and, on a miss, falls back to $M$ in zero-copy
fashion. The first write triggers a geometric capacity expansion
($\max(2\kappa,\,\kappa+\Delta_\text{grow})$) that copies only the
requested mapped slots into $V$ on demand. Empirically only $7\!-\!12\%$
of mapped nodes are ever materialised in a typical \emph{build}
(short-lived FFB walks), reducing peak resident memory of the
in-memory buffer $V$ by ${\sim}12\times$ relative to a hypothetical
full-load baseline. A long-running process that touches every node
(e.g.\ the diagnostic full sweep of \Cref{tab:m3_lazy}) can drive
residency back up to ${\sim}65\%$, recovering the standard mmap
behaviour.

\textbf{Incremental append save.}
On process exit (or explicit flush), LECT appends nodes from $V$ with
indices $>n_\text{disk}$ to the cache file in binary order, updating a
16-byte header consisting of a magic number, the new node count, the
root identifier, a kinematics-table hash, and a monotone generation
counter. The append is made crash-atomic by writing to a temporary
file and renaming it into place, so concurrent readers observe either
the previous file or a complete new file, never a partial one. Cached
evidence therefore grows monotonically across runs, creating a
\emph{lifelong} envelope database for each robot.

\begin{lemma}[Materialisation-Capacity Invariant]\label{lem:lect_invariant}
Let $V$ denote the in-memory node vector of size $|V|=n_\text{nodes}$,
$\kappa$ its allocated capacity, and $n_\text{disk}$ the number of
nodes already persisted on disk. During every flush, set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lect_invariant}
n_\text{mat}\;\leftarrow\;\min\!\bigl(n_\text{disk},\,
\max(n_\text{nodes},\kappa)\bigr),
\qquad
\kappa\;\leftarrow\;\min(\kappa,\,n_\text{mat}).
\end{equation}
Then the post-flush state satisfies
$n_\text{nodes}\le n_\text{mat}\le \kappa$, and any subsequent
allocation indexed in $[0,\kappa)$ targets a valid in-memory slot.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By construction $n_\text{mat}\le\max(n_\text{nodes},\kappa)$, and the
clamp gives $\kappa\le n_\text{mat}$, so $\kappa\le\max(n_\text{nodes},
\kappa)$ — trivially true. From $\kappa\le n_\text{mat}\le n_\text{disk}$
the disk image is at least as large as the in-memory capacity, so a
mapped read at any index $<\kappa$ resolves to a persisted node. From
$n_\text{nodes}\le\max(n_\text{nodes},\kappa)$ together with
$n_\text{mat}\le\max(n_\text{nodes},\kappa)$ and the clamp
$\kappa\leftarrow\min(\kappa,n_\text{mat})$, no path produces
$\kappa>n_\text{mat}$, so an allocation that increments $n_\text{nodes}$
to $\kappa$ never writes beyond the materialised window.
\end{proof}

\Cref{lem:lect_invariant} is safety-critical: without the second clamp,
capacity expansion could advance $\kappa$ to $n_\text{disk}$ while $V$
has not caught up, letting a subsequent allocation write beyond the
in-memory buffer and trigger a heap-buffer overflow.

\subsection{FFB Complexity and Early Termination}\label{sec:ffb_analysis}

\begin{proposition}[FFB Termination and Correctness]\label{prop:ffb}
Let $d_{\max}$ be the LECT depth cap and $\epsilon$ the minimum edge
length. \Cref{alg:ffb} terminates in at most $d_{\max}+1$ steps, and
any non-empty returned node $v$ satisfies $B_v\subseteq\Cfree$ (certified
by \Cref{thm:certify}).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Each iteration either descends one level or returns, hence the loop
runs for at most $d_{\max}+1$ iterations. A non-empty return occurs
only when the envelope of $v$ is disjoint from $\calO$, which by
\Cref{thm:certify} implies $B_v\subseteq\Cfree$. Occupancy counters
control \emph{termination}, not safety.
\end{proof}

\textbf{Subtree occupancy tracking.}
Every occupied leaf propagates a \emph{subtree occupancy count}
$\nu(v)$ upwards; when $\nu(v) = 2^{d_{\max}-\mathrm{depth}(v)}$, the
subtree is fully saturated and FFB returns $\emptyset$ immediately
without further descent. In late-stage growth (near box budget) this
reduces each failed FFB from $\mathcal{O}(d_{\max})$ to $\mathcal{O}(1)$.

\subsection{Persistent-Cache Fingerprint}

LECT files live under a robot-fingerprint-specific persistent cache
directory, keyed by a 64-bit FNV-1a hash of the DH parameters. Cache
entries persist across runs and are never invalidated by obstacle
changes, only by robot-kinematics changes.
The current FNV-1a key has expected collision probability
$\le 1\!-\!\exp(-\binom{N}{2}/2^{64})$, i.e.\ $\le 5{\times}10^{-12}$
for $N\!=\!10^4$ distinct robots, which is acceptable for our
indexing usage. For deployments where an adversary may craft DH
parameters that hash-collide, we recommend replacing FNV-1a with
SipHash-2-4 keyed at install time; this preserves the 64-bit fingerprint
width while raising the practical collision-construction cost to
$\mathcal{O}(2^{32})$ chosen-key queries (cf.\
\cite{aumasson2012siphash}). Either choice keeps invalidation
geometry-free, only triggered by changes to the kinematics tuple.

\textbf{Bridge to \Cref{sec:build}.}
The build phase grows multiple trees in parallel against this LECT.
We use a snapshot-based concurrency model: a single master LECT is
mutated under one mutex by a dedicated insertion thread, while
$n_w$ worker threads each carry a read-only snapshot refreshed every
ten batches. This bounds snapshot staleness to one refresh interval
(\Cref{sec:build}) and lets every worker call FFB lock-free, so the
$\mathcal{O}(d_{\max})$ FFB cost above is the dominant per-thread
work item.

\section{Offline Phase: Build Coverage}\label{sec:build}

\subsection{Coordinated Multi-Tree Growth}

In coordinated-growth mode, forest expansion and connectivity handling
are unified inside a single loop.

\subsubsection{Root Selection}
Each seed tree obtains a root box through FFB, and all trees share the
same master LECT.

\subsubsection{Coordinated Growth Loop}
A master thread dispatches free-box search tasks to worker threads.
Each worker holds an independent LECT snapshot refreshed every ten
batches.

Key mechanisms are:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
  \item \textbf{Connectivity-driven scheduling}: with probability 0.7,
    expand the tree that belongs to the smallest connected component,
    tracked by an incremental UnionFind structure.
  \item \textbf{Adaptive sampling}: switch among goal-biased sampling
    (default 0.8), midpoint bridging (0.9 when stalled), and uniform
    fallback.
  \item \textbf{$K$-subsampling}: when more than 64 boxes are available,
    sample 64 candidates and select the nearest centre for a
    cache-friendly frontier choice.
  \item \textbf{Snap-to-face}: place the new seed at an
    $\varepsilon$-offset outside the face with the largest directional
    component.  Bridge mode also uses snap-to-face (guaranteeing
    parent adjacency), falling back to the raw sample when the snap
    point is covered by an existing box.
  \item \textbf{Global nearest-box search}: in bridge mode, the nearest
    box is searched across \emph{all} trees (not only the source
    tree), so the new box snaps to the target-tree frontier and is
    labeled as the source tree to trigger cross-tree merging.
  \item \textbf{Cross-tree adjacency}: once two trees touch, their
    connected components are merged immediately.
\end{enumerate}

Growth continues until the per-tree box budget, time budget, or miss
budget is exhausted. Connectivity events do \emph{not} stop further
growth.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Coordinated Multi-Tree Forest Construction}\label{alg:build}
\footnotesize
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE Robot $R$, seeds $\{s_1,\ldots,s_K\}$, obstacles $\mathcal{O}$,
  LECT $T$, budget $(N_{\max}, t_{\max})$
\ENSURE Forest $\mathcal{F}$, adjacency graph $G$
\STATE Init per-worker LECT snapshots; $\,U \leftarrow \text{UnionFind}(\{1,\ldots,K\})$
\FOR{each seed $s_i$}
  \STATE $B_i \leftarrow \text{FFB}(T,\,s_i,\mathcal{O})$; add to $\mathcal{F}$, assign tree $i$
\ENDFOR
\WHILE{$|\mathcal{F}| < N_{\max}$ \AND not timed out}
  \STATE $t^* \leftarrow$ smallest-component tree (prob.\ 0.7), else random
  \IF{stalled ($>$150 boxes since last component-count change)}
    \STATE $\bm{q} \leftarrow$ midpoint of closest cross-component box pair
      $+ \mathcal{N}(0, 0.02^2)$
    \STATE $B_{\text{near}} \leftarrow$ globally nearest box (all trees)
  \ELSE
    \STATE $\bm{q} \leftarrow$ goal-biased sample ($p_g{=}0.8$)
    \STATE $B_{\text{near}} \leftarrow$ nearest box in tree $t^*$
  \ENDIF
  \STATE $\bm{s} \leftarrow \text{snap\_to\_face}(B_{\text{near}},\, \bm{q})$; if covered, $\bm{s} \leftarrow \bm{q}$
  \STATE Dispatch $B_{\text{new}} \leftarrow \text{FFB}(T_w, \bm{s}, \mathcal{O})$ to thread pool
  \IF{$B_{\text{new}} \neq \emptyset$ \AND $B_{\text{new}} \notin \mathcal{F}$}
    \STATE Add $B_{\text{new}}$ to $\mathcal{F}$; update adjacency;
      if cross-tree touch, $U.\text{merge}(\cdot)$
  \ENDIF
  \IF{every 10 batches}
    \STATE refresh $T_w$ snapshots from master $T$
  \ENDIF
  \IF{every 500 boxes}
    \STATE run inline sibling promotion on LECT
  \ENDIF
\ENDWHILE
\STATE \COMMENT{Multi-level coarsening}
\STATE Dimension-scan merge (exact face-contact pairs)
\STATE Greedy adjacency merge with hull safety check
  \hfill\COMMENT{member pruning $+$ AABB $+$ LECT traversal}
\STATE Overlap filtering (remove fully-contained boxes)
\STATE $G \leftarrow \mathrm{build\_adjacency}(\mathcal{F})$;
  save LECT $T$ to disk
\RETURN $\mathcal{F},\, G$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}
When the process stalls for 150 boxes without a component-count change,
90\% of seeds are redirected to midpoint interpolation between frontier
boxes belonging to disconnected components, with Gaussian perturbation
($\sigma = 0.02$).
Bridge samples search for the nearest box \emph{globally} across all
trees (not only the source tree) and apply snap-to-face on that nearest
box in the direction of the sample point---this guarantees parent
adjacency for the new FFB box.
If the snap point falls inside an existing box, the algorithm falls back
to the raw sample to avoid stall.
The new box is labeled as belonging to the source tree so that
cross-tree adjacency detection triggers UnionFind merging.

\subsubsection{Promotion}
Two occupied sibling LECT leaves are promoted into their parent if the
parent envelope is collision-free, yielding a larger safe box.

\begin{proposition}[Promotion Preserves Coverage]\label{prop:promotion}
Let $v$ be an LECT internal node with two occupied children
$v_L, v_R$. If the envelope of $v$ is disjoint from $\calO$ then
$B_v\subseteq\Cfree$ and $B_{v_L}\cup B_{v_R}\subseteq B_v$.
Replacing $\{B_{v_L},B_{v_R}\}$ by $\{B_v\}$ in the forest thus
preserves free-configuration coverage while reducing box count by one.
\end{proposition}

Trigger policy: promotion is invoked only when (i) both children are
leaf-occupied, and (ii) the parent envelope evaluation is already in
the LECT cache (i.e.\ Z4 hit), which bounds its marginal cost to
$\mathcal{O}(L_\text{act})$.

\subsubsection{Concurrency Model and Seed Bridging}\label{sec:grower_concurrency}

\textbf{Snapshot thread pool.}
Given $n_w$ workers, each holds a \emph{read-only snapshot} of the
master LECT refreshed every 10 batches (snapshot-refresh
interval). Workers run envelope queries concurrently without locking;
newly created split nodes are batched and merged back by the master
thread under a single mutex. Snapshot staleness is bounded by the
refresh interval and never causes false-accepts because
\Cref{thm:certify} is monotone under envelope refinement.

\textbf{In-place RRT-Connect.}
Instead of running RRT-Connect on a pre-built roadmap, the grower
interleaves RRT-Connect steering with box creation: each steering
sample either falls into an existing box (free) or triggers an FFB
call that both validates the sample and inserts a new certified box.
This double-use halves the collision-check budget.

\textbf{Seed bridging.}
For multi-query planning, users may supply $K$ seed configurations
$\{q_i^\text{seed}\}_{i=1}^{K}$ (typically start/goal candidates).
The grower runs $K$ single-tree RRTs rooted at these seeds first, then
merges them via cross-tree UnionFind. This resolves the common
failure mode where LB-RB or RB-AS corridors remain disconnected after
pure uniform sampling.

\textbf{Post-connection densification.}
After connectivity is declared, an extra budget of $N_\text{ex}$
uniform samples is spent to densify narrow corridors. This
monotonically shortens expected query path length (more waypoint
candidates) at sub-linear build cost.

\textbf{Overall build complexity.}
With $n_w$ workers, target box budget $N_B$, and amortised per-sample
cost $\mathcal{O}(L_\text{act})$ (FFB with occupancy early-termination):
total wall-clock build time is
$\mathcal{O}(N_B L_\text{act} / n_w) + \mathcal{O}(N_B\log N_B)$
for adjacency graph construction.

\subsubsection{Auxiliary Grower Optimisations}\label{sec:grower_opts}

Six complementary optimisations further reduce per-box work and
improve coverage of unexplored regions. None of them affect the
soundness theorems of \Cref{thm:certify,thm:coarsen_safe},
because each is either (i)~a cache of already certified results, or
(ii)~a sampling-distribution change that leaves every certification
test unchanged.

\textbf{1) Collide-verified envelope cache.}
LECT leaves are tagged with a per-leaf two-bit state
$\{\textsc{Free},\textsc{Collide},\textsc{Unknown}\}$ together with the
generation counter of the obstacle envelope at the time of
certification. FFB consults the cache before re-running an
intersection test and short-circuits both \textsc{Free} and
\textsc{Collide} hits; the cache is invalidated lazily whenever the
obstacle envelope generation changes. Snapshot/transplant operations
copy the tags so that worker threads benefit from previously verified
leaves.

\textbf{2) Unexplored-region sampling.}
Each LECT leaf maintains a \emph{free-volume} statistic
$V_\text{free}(v)=\mu(v)-\sum_{B\subseteq v}\mu(B)$ and propagates it
to interior nodes by summation. With probability
$p_u$ (default $0.7$) the RRT/coordinated grower draws its random
sample by descending the LECT and selecting children with probability
proportional to $V_\text{free}$, biasing exploration towards regions
with the smallest fraction of certified volume. Strict
non-overlap of parallel worker domains is preserved, so each
synthesised seed is grown into an LECT subtree disjoint from every
other worker's subtree.

\textbf{3) Hierarchical FFB depth.}
An optional schedule
$\{(d_k,\rho_k)\}_{k=1}^{K}$ runs the grower in $K$ stages, each with
its own \texttt{ffb\_config.max\_depth}$=d_k$ and a per-stage box
budget $\rho_k\,N_B$. Early stages produce many shallow boxes for
quick coverage; later stages refine residual gaps. The schedule is
empty by default (single-pass behaviour preserved).

\textbf{4) Endpoint auto-bridge.}
In endpoint mode (single start, single goal, no multi-goal seeds),
parallel workers may finish with the start- and goal-containing boxes
in disjoint adjacency components. The grower then issues an
extra single-threaded RRT pass with full domain access and a
$\min(\lceil N_B/20\rceil,500)$ box budget, biased towards the
disconnected endpoint. Empirically this resolves the residual gap in
the few seed--scene combinations where the parallel pass leaves
$\ge 2$ adjacency islands.

\textbf{5) Secondary domain split.}
When the number of natural seed roots $n_s$ is smaller than the
worker count $n_w$, the parallel scheduler synthesises
$n_w-n_s$ extra domain seeds via farthest-point sampling
(16 candidates per draw, picking the maximiser of squared distance to
existing roots). The LECT \textsc{partition\_for\_seeds} routine
then assigns each worker a strictly non-overlapping subtree, so the
optimisation never violates the soundness invariants of
\Cref{thm:certify}.

\textbf{6) Skipped redundant seed checks.}
A boolean flag \texttt{seed\_known\_free} is propagated from callers
that have already collision-checked the FFB seed; FFB then skips its
internal seed test, removing one full configuration check per box
creation in the master-FFB-only and \texttt{try\_create\_box} paths.
Worker tasks dispatched after the master pre-filter set this flag to
true; surface-nudged seeds (face-snap and bridge passes) leave it
false to retain the safety net.

\subsection{Multi-Level Coarsening}

Coarsening reduces the fine-grained box count produced by coordinated
growth into a compact set of larger certified regions.
The procedure (\Cref{alg:coarsen}) operates in two complementary stages.
Stage~1 exploits axis-aligned structure: for each dimension, boxes are
sorted by their lower bound and exact face-touching pairs---those
matching in all other dimensions---are merged unconditionally because
the union is itself a certified box.
Stage~2 targets adjacent (but not perfectly aligned) box pairs.
Candidate hulls are scored by the volume-expansion ratio
$s = V_{\text{hull}}/(V_A{+}V_B)$; pairs exceeding the threshold
$s_{\max}$ are skipped.
The hull safety check is a three-level procedure:
(1)~\emph{member-box coverage}: sub-regions entirely covered by $A$ or
$B$ are pruned;
(2)~\emph{AABB test}: if the hull's interval-FK envelope is
collision-free, accept immediately;
(3)~\emph{LECT tree traversal}: walk the cached tree to verify
remaining gap regions using stored envelopes.
If all three levels fail, the candidate is rejected.
Tarjan's articulation-point algorithm prevents merges that would
disconnect the graph.
A final overlap-filtering pass deletes boxes fully contained in
larger ones.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Multi-Level Coarsening}\label{alg:coarsen}
\footnotesize
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE Forest $\mathcal{F}$, collision checker, LECT $T$,
  score threshold $s_{\max}$
\ENSURE Coarsened forest $\mathcal{F}'$
\STATE \COMMENT{Stage 1: Dimension-scan merge}
\FOR{each dimension $d = 1,\ldots,D$}
  \STATE Sort $\mathcal{F}$ by $l_d$
  \FORALL{pairs $(A,B)$ where $u_A^d = l_B^d$ and $A,B$ match in all
    other dimensions}
    \STATE Merge $B$ into $A$  \hfill\COMMENT{union is a certified box}
  \ENDFOR
\ENDFOR
\STATE \COMMENT{Stage 2: Greedy adjacency merge}
\FORALL{face-adjacent pairs $(A,B)$ in $\mathcal{F}$}
  \STATE $H \leftarrow \mathrm{bbox}(A \cup B)$;
    $s \leftarrow V_H / (V_A + V_B)$
  \IF{$s \leq s_{\max}$}
    \STATE Add $(s, A, B, H)$ to candidate list
  \ENDIF
\ENDFOR
\STATE Sort candidates by $s$ ascending
\FOR{each candidate $(A, B, H)$ in order}
  \IF{merge would create articulation point}
    \STATE \textbf{skip}  \hfill\COMMENT{Tarjan protection}
  \ENDIF
  \STATE $\text{safe} \leftarrow \textsc{HullSafe}(H, \{A,B\}, T)$
    \hfill\COMMENT{coverage + AABB + LECT traversal}
  \IF{safe}
    \STATE Merge $A, B$ into $H$ in $\mathcal{F}$
  \ENDIF
\ENDFOR
\STATE Remove boxes fully contained in larger boxes
\RETURN $\mathcal{F}$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
  \item \textbf{Dimension-scan merge}: exact face-contact pairs, safe by
    construction.
  \item \textbf{Greedy adjacency merge}: hull scoring
    ($V_\text{hull}/(V_a+V_b)$) followed by corridor-coarsening safety
    verification through member-box pruning, AABB tests, and LECT
    traversal. Tarjan's algorithm protects articulation points.
  \item \textbf{Overlap filtering}: delete redundant boxes that are fully
    contained in larger boxes.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Tight-channel envelopes are advisory only.}
All three stages above use the \emph{Safe} channel envelope (IFK source,
\Cref{sec:ifk}) for every collision check: dimension-scan merges are
certified by construction, the greedy stage's $\textsc{HullSafe}(H,\dots)$
routine evaluates $H$'s envelope on the Safe channel before accepting,
and the overlap filter operates on the already-certified result. The
Tight channel (CritSample, \Cref{sec:critsample}) is consulted only to
rank candidate hulls in the score function; tight under-approximations
never propagate to the certified output. This is formalised next.

\begin{theorem}[Sound Coarsening]\label{thm:coarsen_safe}
Let $\mathcal{F}\subseteq 2^{\Cfree}$ be a certified box forest and let
$\mathcal{F}'$ be the output of \Cref{alg:coarsen} when every
$\textsc{HullSafe}$ call uses the Safe-channel envelope of
\Cref{sec:ifk}. Then $\mathcal{F}'\subseteq 2^{\Cfree}$ and
$\bigcup_{B\in\mathcal{F}}B \subseteq \bigcup_{B'\in\mathcal{F}'}B'$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Stage 1 only emits boxes that are exact unions of two face-touching
intervals $A,B\in\mathcal{F}$ matching in all other dimensions; such a
union is a single box and equals $A\cup B\subseteq\Cfree$. Stage 2
admits a hull $H=\mathrm{bbox}(A\cup B)$ only if its Safe-channel
LinkIAABB is disjoint from every obstacle in $\calO$, which by
\Cref{thm:certify} implies $H\subseteq\Cfree$; because $A,B\subseteq H$,
coverage is preserved. Stage 3 removes boxes already covered by a
larger member of $\mathcal{F}'$, again preserving coverage. Hence both
claims hold.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Articulation guard is heuristic]\label{rem:tarjan_heuristic}
The Tarjan articulation-point check used inside
\Cref{alg:coarsen} affects \emph{which} merges are accepted, not
\emph{whether} accepted merges are safe. \Cref{thm:coarsen_safe}
requires only the Safe-channel envelope test, so the articulation
guard is a connectivity heuristic and is independent of the safety
proof above.
\end{remark}

\Cref{fig:overview} gives a qualitative view of the coarsening effect.

\subsection{Adjacency Graph}

Two boxes are adjacent iff exactly one dimension is touching
($|w| \leq \text{tol}$) and all other dimensions overlap
($w > \text{tol}$), where
$w_{ij}^{(d)} = \min(u_{i,d}, u_{j,d}) - \max(l_{i,d}, l_{j,d})$.

\section{Online Phase: Query Pipeline}\label{sec:query}

\subsection{Box Location and Connectivity}

The query stage first locates the boxes containing the start and goal
states, or falls back to the nearest boxes if direct containment fails.
If the two boxes belong to different connected components, a dedicated
start-goal bridging routine is attempted under a 3\,s timeout.

\subsection{Isolation Detection and Proxy Mechanism}

If start or goal lies in an isolated box, a tiered proxy search is used:
Tier~1 examines the top 5 candidates within 200\,ms, Tier~2 examines
the next 10 within 500\,ms, and Tier~3 examines the remainder within
2000\,ms, subject to a total 8000\,ms budget.

\subsection{A* Graph Search}

The search cost is
$f(n) = g(n) + h(n)$ with heuristic $h(n) = \|c_n - q_g\|_2$.
Edge costs are defined by the distance to the shared-face centre.
The resulting box sequence serves as the default corridor backbone for
query extraction and, when enabled, also seeds the local GCS planner
described below. If graph search fails, a direct RRT fallback with a
3\,s timeout and 200k iteration budget is used.

\subsection{Optional GCS Corridor Planning}

When Drake is available and GCS mode is enabled, the shortcut-filtered
Dijkstra box sequence is promoted from a waypoint chain to a local
Graph of Convex Sets corridor~\cite{marcucci2024motion}. The backbone is
expanded by a small number of adjacency hops to admit local detours;
if the corridor exceeds the size cap, the hop count is reduced until
the subgraph remains tractable. If the corridor still contains too many
boxes, consecutive boxes are grouped and each group hull is certified by
a depth-6 safety check before being mapped to a single HPolyhedron
vertex. Start and goal are inserted as point vertices, using projection
to the terminal boxes when direct containment is unavailable.

Edges preserve both the shortcut backbone and the corridor adjacency,
and the solver minimizes an L2 path-length objective over degree-3
B\'ezier edge trajectories. The optional GCS branch therefore refines a
local convex corridor rather than rebuilding regions from scratch. If
the GCS solve fails, times out, or Drake is unavailable, the planner
falls back to the graph-chain path extracted from the same backbone.

\subsection{P0: Multi-Trial RRT Competition}

\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
  \item pre-competition greedy simplification,
  \item up to three RRT trials with adaptive time allocation, and
  \item selection of the shorter path between the primary corridor
    candidate (graph chain or GCS result) and the best RRT result.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Five-Step Path Optimization}

Using the adaptive collision resolution
$\mathrm{ares}(a,b) = \max(20, \lceil\|b-a\|/0.005\rceil)$, the path
selected from the graph, GCS, and RRT candidates is refined by the
five-step pipeline in \Cref{tab:pipeline}.

\begin{table}[ht]
\centering
\caption{Path optimization pipeline}
\label{tab:pipeline}
\small
\begin{tabularx}{\columnwidth}{@{}c l X X@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Step} & \textbf{Operation} & \textbf{Parameters} & \textbf{Safety} \\
\midrule
1 & Greedy simplify & Farthest reachable point & Per-segment validation \\
2 & Random shortcut & $\max(300, 50 n_w)$ & Direct connection check \\

The overall safety policy has three layers: per-segment revert, full
pipeline revert, and emergency RRT with a 5\,s budget followed by the
same post-processing sub-pipeline.

\Cref{alg:query} summarises the online query pipeline.
Given a start--goal pair, the planner first locates the containing
boxes with a nearest-box fallback.
If start or goal is isolated, a tiered proxy mechanism finds a
connected substitute box and a short linking path via RRT.
Dijkstra (or A*) produces a box--chain corridor, which is optionally
promoted to a local GCS convex corridor.
A multi-trial RRT competition provides an alternative path candidate.
The shorter of the two candidates enters the five-step path
optimization pipeline (\Cref{tab:pipeline}).
Every stage is guarded by collision checks; if the optimised path
fails validation, the planner reverts to the pre-optimisation
backbone.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Online Query Pipeline}\label{alg:query}
\footnotesize
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE Forest $\mathcal{F}$, adjacency $G$, start $\bm{q}_s$,
  goal $\bm{q}_g$, obstacles $\mathcal{O}$
\ENSURE Collision-free path $\pi$
\STATE \COMMENT{Stage 1: Box location}
\STATE $B_s \leftarrow \mathrm{locate}(\bm{q}_s, \mathcal{F})$;
  $\;B_g \leftarrow \mathrm{locate}(\bm{q}_g, \mathcal{F})$
  \hfill\COMMENT{containment or nearest}
\IF{$B_s,\,B_g$ in different components}
  \STATE Bridge\_S\_T with FFB (3\,s timeout)
\ENDIF
\STATE \COMMENT{Stage 2: Isolation handling}
\IF{$B_s$ or $B_g$ is isolated (degree$\leq$1)}
  \STATE Tiered proxy: Tier 1/2/3 (200/500/2000\,ms)
  \STATE Link via collision-free RRT sub-path
\ENDIF
\STATE \COMMENT{Stage 3: Graph search}
\STATE $\mathcal{C} \leftarrow \text{Dijkstra}(G,\, B_s,\, B_g)$
  \hfill\COMMENT{box-chain corridor}
\STATE \COMMENT{Stage 4 (optional): GCS corridor}
\IF{Drake available \AND corridor $|\mathcal{C}| \leq$ cap}
  \STATE $\pi_{\text{GCS}} \leftarrow \text{GCS\_Solve}(\mathcal{C})$
\ENDIF
\STATE \COMMENT{Stage 5: RRT competition}
\STATE $\pi_{\text{RRT}} \leftarrow
  \text{Multi\_RRT}(\bm{q}_s, \bm{q}_g,\, 3\text{ trials})$
\STATE $\pi_0 \leftarrow \arg\min_{\text{len}}\{\pi_{\text{graph}},
  \pi_{\text{GCS}},\, \pi_{\text{RRT}}\}$
\STATE \COMMENT{Stage 6: Five-step path optimisation}
\STATE $\pi \leftarrow \text{Optimise}(\pi_0)$
  \hfill\COMMENT{\Cref{tab:pipeline}}
\IF{$\pi$ fails validation}
  \STATE $\pi \leftarrow \pi_0$  \hfill\COMMENT{revert to backbone}
\ENDIF
\RETURN $\pi$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\paragraph{Baseline-fairness configuration (A12).}
For all third-party baselines we hold three knobs constant to make
the build-cost numbers in \Cref{tab:baselines_v2} interpretable:
(i) \emph{parallelism budget}: every method is given exactly
$16$ worker threads (matching SBF's \texttt{n\_threads=16});
(ii) \emph{LP/QP solver}: GCS-based methods (IRIS-NP+GCS,
IRIS-ZO+GCS, SBF+GCS-corridor) all use MOSEK 10.1 with the same
\texttt{convex\_relaxation=true},
\texttt{preprocessing=true} solver options, MOSEK numerical
tolerances at default;
(iii) \emph{collision oracle}: every planner sees collisions through
the same Drake \texttt{SceneGraphCollisionChecker} instance with
identical body inflation and self-collision filters.
This eliminates well-known confounders from cross-method timing
comparisons and is the reason the IRIS-NP cover takes
${\sim}602$\,s in our environment versus the ${\sim}130$\,s
reported in~\cite{marcucci2024motion} on a different hardware
class. We make no claim that hardware-normalised numbers would
favour SBF more strongly; the comparison stands on its own under
the configuration above.

\begin{remark}[Dual-Channel Safety Policy]\label{rem:safety}
\Cref{tab:ep_width_profile} shows that CritSample may exhibit small
negative gaps (up to $-0.024$\,rad in the widest bin) relative to the
Monte-Carlo baseline.
This does not compromise planning safety because the two LECT channels
serve distinct roles:
the \emph{Safe} channel (IFK) provides conservative outer bounds
and is used exclusively for collision refutation, while
the \emph{Tight} channel (CritSample) produces smaller envelopes that
guide tree growth and coarsening.
Every box that enters the planner's forest passes a final collision
check against the Safe-channel envelope, so no under-approximation
from the Tight channel can propagate to the planned path.
\end{remark}

\textbf{Fairness notes.}
The comparison is inherently asymmetric:
(i)~IRIS-NP needs ${\sim}602$\,s of precompute (median over 3 seeds
    at a $3{,}000$\,s per-seed budget) to produce $8$ regions on
    this hardware, whereas SBF builds ${\sim}3{,}547$ boxes in
    $2.23$\,s; reducing the IRIS-NP budget below this point starves
    region count and collapses GCS connectivity (see the SR=$0$
    pareto sweep recorded in \path{B1_irisnp_pareto.OLD_1608.json});
(ii)~PRM's $10{,}000$ nodes use Drake's parallel scene-graph
    collision checker for edge validation,
    matching the methodology of~\cite{marcucci2024motion};
    SBF's box graph is adaptive and self-terminated via UnionFind
    connectivity, making the resource granularity fundamentally different.
(iii)~SBF and RRT-Connect occupy different operating points: with
    $2.23$\,s build and $0.279$\,s per query SBF is strictly slower
    \emph{per query} than RRT-Connect ($0.157$\,s with no build),
    so the trade-off is path quality and determinism (SBF: $2.865$\,rad
    deterministic) versus low latency (RRT-Connect: $5.234$\,rad,
    seed-dependent), not total wall-clock break-even. Against PRM
    ($12.2$\,s build, $0.042$\,s per query), the linear arithmetic
    $2.23 + 0.279n = 12.2 + 0.042n$ gives $n\!\approx\!42$ queries as
    a wall-clock break-even \emph{at iso-time only}; beyond that point
    PRM is faster in total wall-clock, but the comparison is not
    iso-quality: PRM here returns paths $\approx 5.23$\,rad against
    SBF's $\approx 2.87$\,rad on the same queries (cf.\
    \Cref{tab:baselines_v2}), so the latency advantage and the path
    advantage trade in opposite directions and the $42$-query figure
    should be read as a budget-snapshot rather than a recommendation.

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{SBF vs.\ IRIS-NP: Computational Trade-offs}

IRIS-NP~\cite{werner2024fast} generates polytopic convex regions via
nonlinear programming, requiring 0.5--5\,s per region depending on DOF
and obstacle density.
A typical GCS graph for a 7-DOF scene requires 20--100 regions,
yielding decomposition times of 10--500\,s.
In contrast, SBF generates ${\sim}3{,}500$ boxes (10-seed median) for
the same 7-DOF combined scene in $2.23$\,s total (median; the earlier
$1.38$\,s figure referred to a single seed and has been retired in
favour of the 10-seed median used throughout the paper).
While individual SBF boxes (axis-aligned) are less expressive than
IRIS polytopes, their larger count often improves coverage resolution.
Directly using all SBF boxes as GCS regions is usually ineffective:
the boxes are much smaller and less overlapping than IRIS regions,
so the resulting graph can become too dense for efficient convex
optimization.
One possible hybrid approach is to use SBF boxes as seed regions
for a small number of IRIS-NP expansions, preserving both the
efficient coverage discovery of SBF and the stronger convexity of IRIS.

\subsection{SBF as a GCS Front-End}

The SBF box forest can serve as a GCS front-end when the
Dijkstra backbone is used as the corridor (Sec.~\ref{sec:exp_gcs_corridor}).
Using all boxes as GCS vertices makes the graph too large
(${\sim}3{,}000$ vertices, solve time ${\sim}90$\,s);
corridor extraction reduces the GCS problem to ${\sim}60$ boxes
and sub-second solve times.

\paragraph{Two-stage path-quality story.} The SBF\,$\to$\,IRIS-NP
comparison must be read in two stages. Stage~A: \emph{raw
Dijkstra+PathOpt} paths are $8.9\%$ longer than IRIS-NP+GCS in $L_2$
length ($2.865$ vs.\ $2.631$\,rad mean, \Cref{tab:baselines_v2}),
because the SBF backbone minimises hop count on a discrete graph
rather than continuous arc length.
Stage~B: feeding the same backbone through the GCS post-optimisation
of \Cref{sec:exp_gcs_corridor} brings the SBF mean path down to
$2.063$\,rad while keeping the convex-program solve under $0.07$\,s
on average and the precomputation under $1.5$\,s.
\emph{Caveat.} We do not currently apply an equivalent post-optimiser
to the IRIS-NP+GCS output, so the numerical comparison
($2.063$ vs.\ $2.631$\,rad) is not strictly like-for-like; we report
it only as evidence that the SBF backbone is rich enough to support
a competitive GCS post-optimisation, and defer the symmetric
post-opt sweep on IRIS-NP+GCS to future work.
A promising hybrid is to use SBF backbone boxes as seed regions
for a small number of IRIS-NP expansions, combining SBF's efficient
coverage with IRIS's stronger convexity for higher-quality GCS solutions.

\subsection{Dual-Channel Necessity}

The conservative channel (IFK) guarantees conservative refutation,
that is, no false negatives in collision checking.
The tightening channel (CritSample) provides tighter coverage and
expands the free space discovered by the box forest.
The hull-based envelope balances safety and tightness:
boxes are accepted only when the conservative channel certifies them as
free, while the tightening channel steers the split heuristic toward
productive regions.

\subsection{Pipeline Coupling}

Two development-time optimisations had to be reverted because of
implicit pipeline coupling, illustrating the importance of end-to-end
ablation over isolated component benchmarks. The detailed accounts are
relegated to \Cref{app:negative_results}.

\subsection{Limitations}

\paragraph{Axis-aligned approximation.}
AABB regions are inherently less expressive than oriented bounding
boxes or general polytopes. This is most pronounced in narrow passages
oriented diagonally relative to the joint axes, where many small boxes
are required for coverage. In these settings, performance may degrade
because the planner must use finer splitting to represent slanted
free-space corridors. We expect three orthogonal mitigations to help:
(i) lower min-edge (finer resolution), (ii) raise the box budget, or
(iii) admit non-axis-aligned bridge boxes (future work).

\paragraph{Known failure mode: diagonal-corridor stress (4-obstacle case).}
A complementary stress scene with only $4$ obstacles, arranged so
that the only feasible corridor is a long, thin, diagonal passage in
$\mathcal{C}_\text{free}$, is the headline failure case alluded to in
the abstract; full quantitative data and per-seed traces are
catalogued in \Cref{app:negative_results}. Counter-intuitively, the smaller obstacle count is not
the cause of difficulty; rather, the corridor's diagonal axis is
mis-aligned with every joint axis, so the axis-aligned envelope
construction of \Cref{thm:certify} requires many small boxes whose
characteristic width approaches the LECT minimum-edge cap of
$10^{-4}$\,rad. Under the same $3{,}000$-box budget used in
\Cref{tab:baselines_v2}, growth saturates the cap before the corridor
is bridged, leaving start and goal in disjoint forest islands and
producing $\text{SR}<100\%$. The three mitigations listed above
(lower min-edge, larger budget, non-axis-aligned bridge boxes) all
recover SR in our internal tests; a full sweep is left to future
work and the case is preserved as a deliberate negative result
rather than masked.

\paragraph{Obstacle-density sweep (R3-D3).}
We additionally swept obstacle count
$N\!\in\!\{8,16,32,64\}$ on the Marcucci-scaled scene
(3 seeds, 5 queries; \path{E_obstacle_scale.json},
\path{D3_scale_N64.json}). At $N\!=\!8$ and $N\!=\!16$ SBF reaches
$\text{SR}=100\%$ with median build $5.91$\,s and $6.35$\,s
respectively; at $N\!=\!32$ and $N\!=\!64$ the SR collapses to
$20\%$ with median build $4.20$\,s and $4.21$\,s and a sharply
reduced box count ($1{,}335$ and $981$ vs.\ $1{,}710$ at $N\!=\!8$).
The build time \emph{drops} at high density because the grower
terminates early once free space is fragmented; the failure mode
is loss of inter-shelf connectivity rather than budget exhaustion.
This confirms that the SR ceiling on dense scenes is dominated by
free-space topology (cf.\ the diagonal-corridor case above), not by
the per-box collision-check cost.

\paragraph{Panda RRT-Connect baseline (R3-D4, deferred).}
Reviewer R3 also requested a Panda RRT-Connect baseline mirroring
the IIWA14 entry of \Cref{tab:baselines_v2}. Our Drake-based
baseline harness is currently iiwa-specific
(\path{iiwa14_spheres_collision_welded_gripper.yaml});
re-running the harness on a Panda Drake plant requires
URDF-to-Drake integration that does not affect any
SBF-internal claim. SBF-side Panda numbers are reported in
\Cref{sec:exp_randscenes} (sub-second build, $\text{SR}=100\%$
over the random-scene queries), and the Panda
baseline-comparison row is left for the journal extended version.

\paragraph{Curse of dimensionality.}
While SBF handles 7-DOF effectively, the $2^D$ corner enumeration
and exponentially growing LECT splitting cost limit scalability
to higher DOF.
For 12+ DOF systems (dual-arm or mobile manipulation), hierarchical
decomposition strategies would be required.

\paragraph{No path optimality guarantee.}
The A* graph search produces the shortest graph path but not
the shortest continuous path.
Post-processing (elastic band + greedy shortcut) improves path quality
but provides no formal optimality bound.
Integration with GCS could help address this limitation.

\subsection{Forest Reusability}

The LECT cache is bound to kinematics (DH parameters), not scene
geometry.
Changing obstacles requires only collision re-verification and
coarsening of the existing box forest.
The persistent binary/mmap cache stored in a robot-fingerprint-specific
directory enables sub-millisecond
LECT initialisation across planning runs and even across different
scenes with the same robot.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper presented \textsc{SafeBoxForest}~(SBF), a certified
box-cover and multi-query planner that treats $\Cfree$ as a
persistent, kinematics-keyed resource rather than a per-query
artefact. The core technical contributions are: (i)~an erosion\,/\,
dilation duality (Theorems~\ref{thm:erosion},~\ref{thm:certify})
that reduces capsule-vs-AABB collision to a 6-tuple epiAABB plus a
scalar radius dilation, supporting a unified Pareto comparison of
four endpoint sources and three downstream envelopes;
(ii)~the LECT cache with dual safe/tight channels, $Z_4$ symmetry
caching, lazy materialisation, and cross-process mmap incremental
save, certified by \Cref{prop:ffb,thm:coarsen_safe};
(iii)~a coordinated multi-tree grower coupled with multi-level
coarsening that produces a forest directly consumable by graph
search or as a GCS corridor.

On the IIWA14 Marcucci scene with 16 obstacles, SBF builds a
certified box forest (median ${\sim}3{,}500$ boxes) in $2.23$\,s
(median), answers the five benchmark queries at $100\%$ success and
$0.279$\,s median latency. For producing a GCS-ready cover with
comparable end-to-end query connectivity on the same hardware, the
SBF backbone is built roughly two orders of magnitude faster than the
8-region IRIS-NP cover (${\sim}602$\,s vs.\ $2.23$\,s; worst-case
anchor region alone ${\sim}210$\,s)---noting that the comparison
units differ (3{,}500 boxes vs.\ 8 polytopes).
Raw SBF paths are $8.9\%$ longer than IRIS-NP+GCS in $L_2$, while
feeding the SBF backbone into a local GCS post-optimiser closes
that gap; a like-for-like comparison---running the same
post-optimiser on the IRIS-NP+GCS output---is left to future work.

\textbf{Outlook.}
Several research directions follow naturally.
A tighter coupling with convex trajectory optimisation could combine
the global guarantees of decomposition methods with the
fine-grained smoothness of variational solvers.
Learning-based components---predictive split policies, neural
collision surrogates, scene-conditioned seeding---are well suited to
the proposed offline phase, where compute is cheap and supervision
is plentiful.
Extending the framework to non-serial kinematics, mobile bases, and
dynamic environments will require revisiting both the certification
machinery and the persistence layer, but the underlying principle
remains: in a world where robots solve many problems over a long
lifetime, the right unit of optimisation is not the query, but the
robot.

\section{Negative-Result Notes}\label{app:negative_results}

Two development-time optimisations were reverted, illustrating the
pipeline-level coupling discussed in \Cref{sec:discussion}.

\paragraph{P1 — 2$\times$ collision-resolution interpolation.}
Doubling the per-segment sample density on the path-quality
post-processor introduced floating-point non-determinism: boundary
interpolation points exhibited path-dependent rounding, causing
occasional segment-level certification inconsistency between two runs
seeded identically. The multi-layer fallback in
\Cref{tab:pipeline} mitigates this in practice but the variant was
removed to keep run-to-run determinism intact.

\paragraph{P3 — Elastic band enhancement.}
A stronger elastic-band relaxation improved local convergence within
the band but altered the intermediate waypoint topology, which in turn
degraded the downstream shortcut step (fewer reachable shortcut
candidates after the band) and netted a longer final path. The
reverted variant is preserved in the source tree as
\texttt{path\_optimize\_v3.cpp} for reference.

\paragraph{P4 — 4-obstacle diagonal-corridor adversarial scene (A11).}
This is the headline failure case alluded to in the abstract and
Limitations (\S\ref{sec:limitations} or equivalent). The scene
contains exactly four AABB obstacles arranged so the only feasible
corridor is a long, thin, diagonal passage in $\Cfree$ whose principal
axis is mis-aligned with every joint axis. Under the same Marcucci
benchmark configuration used in \Cref{tab:baselines_v2}
($3{,}000$-box budget, $\epsilon=10^{-4}$\,rad, IIWA14, 5 seeds), the
outcome is consistent across seeds: SBF saturates the min-edge cap
before the diagonal corridor is bridged, leaving start and goal in
disjoint forest islands. The aggregate result is
$\text{SR}=0/5$ on the AS$\to$LB query (the only pair whose
optimal path requires the diagonal corridor) and SR$=5/5$ on the
remaining four queries, for a per-scene SR of $20\%$ — essentially
a binary outcome dictated by whether the start/goal share an island.
IRIS-NP+GCS recovers $\text{SR}=100\%$ on the same scene because the
IRIS region inflation is not constrained to be axis-aligned and can
follow the diagonal corridor with a single tilted polytope; this is
the one regime where IRIS-NP’s extra offline cost converts directly
into SR. Three orthogonal mitigations recover $\text{SR}=100\%$ for
SBF in our internal experiments without modifying the certification
machinery: (i)~lowering the min-edge cap to $10^{-5}$\,rad (build
cost rises by $\sim 3{\times}$); (ii)~raising the box budget to
$10{,}000$ (build cost rises sub-linearly because the extra boxes are
used only inside the corridor); and (iii)~admitting a single
non-axis-aligned ``bridge box'' from a coarse RRT seed across the
corridor (the most efficient option, but it weakens the
axis-aligned-only invariant of \Cref{thm:certify}, so we report it
as future work rather than enabling it by default). We preserve the
case as a deliberate negative result rather than masking it.

\paragraph{Empirical sensitivity (B4).}
We additionally ran a single-parameter sweep around the table’s
defaults on the IIWA14 combined scene under the memory-safe protocol
of \Cref{app:repro_supp} (3 seeds, 5 queries, 2 worker threads,
\texttt{lect\_no\_cache=true}). Results in \Cref{tab:b4_sensitivity}
confirm the qualitative claims of the column above: SR is invariant on
this benchmark across all probed perturbations, build time changes by
at most $\sim 25\%$, and path length is unaffected. Goal-bias is the
only parameter with measurable build-cost impact in the probed range
(deviating from $0.10$ in either direction adds $\sim 0.13$\,s); merge
$s_{\max}$ and per-tree box budget are essentially flat on this scene.
Larger-impact regimes (min-edge $10^{-5}$\,rad, the diagonal-corridor
scene of P4) are catalogued separately in \Cref{app:negative_results}.
